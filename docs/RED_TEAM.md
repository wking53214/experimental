# Red team, 2026-10-14: what six hostile reviewers found, what was verified, what changed

**Why.** Through eleven rounds one party built the system, wrote the attackers, tuned the baseline, scored the predictions and wrote the claims. Pre-registration limits how much a scorer can fool itself about results; it does nothing about the questions never asked. Before another round of results, the evidence was attacked.

**Method.** Six independent reviewers, each started fresh with the code, the data, the scoring scripts and a hostile brief, and none with the author's conclusions. Areas: the two-way baseline's fairness (B), the synthetic traces (T), the scoring and aggregation (M), the Python model against the Rust kernel (K), the pre-registration history (P), and the invariant itself under attack (S). Every finding below was then checked by the author: a finding counts as **confirmed** only when it reproduced on the author's own rerun or reading, **plausible** when the reviewer's run is credible but was not independently repeated, **not reproduced** otherwise. The reviewers' full reports, attack scripts and harnesses are not in the repository; the numbers quoted here were recomputed from the committed JSON or rerun.

Severity: **high** means a headline claim is wrong or unsupported; **medium** means overstated or an undisclosed dependency; **low** is cosmetic or bookkeeping.

## B. The two-way baseline

| ID | Finding | Severity | Status | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **The direction's only measured benefit was a cold-start artefact.** The rate rule divides traps by `min(tasks so far, 2000)`, so in slice 0 five traps suffice (2%) where from slice 8 twenty are needed (1%). Every pulser in the committed rosters starts pulsing in slice 0, and that is where the ratchet caught them. With the first pulse moved to slice 7, the ratchet's saving on the period-8 pulser fell from 17% to 2% (tightened in 4 of 20 seeds); period 16 at phase 15 fell from 10% to 0%. An attacker that is quiet for its first 2,000 tasks is never caught at steady state (0.6% of the window, under the rule). | high | confirmed: the reviewer's rerun reproduced every committed number for the author's policies, and the author recomputed its new cells from its JSON | Round 12a reruns this with a phase parameter in the generator (`phase_v1` roster), predictions first. |
| B2 | **A two-way limiter with hysteresis removes every cell where the direction mattered.** Giving the author's capped, drift-holding two-way limiter three things it never had (an increase only after 16 consecutive calm slices, one step per five traps as the ratchet takes, and the expected-load hook) matched the ratchet's pooled honest cost (2.07 against 1.99) and matched or beat it on every attacker on three rosters, including the phase-shifted pulsers (18% and 11% where the ratchet saves 2% and 0%), with zero human decisions. The pulse formula silently assumed an increase on every calm slice. | high | confirmed from the reviewer's JSON; to be replicated in round 12a | `HysteresisController` added to the simulator as policies `twoway_h8` and `twoway_h16`. |
| B3 | The baseline's decrease trigger (3 traps in a 250-task slice) is four times more sensitive than the ratchet's steady-state rule; it fires on `healthy-tail` in 20 of 20 seeds against the ratchet's 7. The baseline also lacked the expected-load hook and took one step per slice however bad the slice. The author conceded the cap and the drift hold; these were not conceded. | medium | confirmed by reading and the committed JSON | Documented here and in POSITIONING. |
| B4 | The "1.99 against 1.99" honest-agent match is 97% two slow-from-start agents cut identically by cap arithmetic. Excluding them, the ratchet costs 0.07 and the capped two-way 0.15. The 17.75 for plain AIMD is likewise mostly those two agents ground to the floor. | medium | confirmed from the committed JSON | Round 12 reports pooled cost with and without the slow-from-start pair. |
| B5 | The formula predicted 18 of 22 sweep cells under the author's own 8-point criterion, not "every cell"; 9 of the 16 round-10 cells were already known from round 9 when it was written. | medium | confirmed | Corrected in POSITIONING and the plan. |

## T. The synthetic traces

| ID | Finding | Severity | Status | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **The honest-agent results depend on deadline headroom.** Healthy medians sit 4 to 17 times under the 100 ms deadline. At 60 ms (three times the slowest healthy median) healthy and tail agents were tightened in 40 of 70 agent-seeds instead of 4, pooled honest cost went from 2.0 to 8.3, and every honest 3x regression was tightened at 9 to 16 points because the excess release fires. Saving stayed 26% by construction. | high | confirmed on fresh seeds (author's rerun at 60 ms) | Rosters `headroom75_v1` and `headroom60_v1`; round 12b. |
| T2 | **"Saved%" is the breaker's arithmetic.** A hung task costs exactly the deadline in both the static and governed runs, and every generated hang (300 to 900 ms) overruns every deadline the governor can set, so saved% equals 1 - 0.9^k for any workload. It measures step size times steps, never the design against a workload. Priced in absolute time (hung time saved minus the legitimate work the tightened deadline destroys), the recommended policy was a net loss of 3.6 to 5.3% of all task time on three of four committed rosters, and the pulse and low-and-slow attackers that carry the direction claim are net losses to tighten. | high | confirmed by reading the burn arithmetic; net-time numbers plausible (reviewer's harness, consistent with the author's smoke run) | Net-time fields (`net_burn_ns`, `net_rerun_ns`, `legit_kills`) added to every outcome; round 12 reports them. |
| T3 | The drift rule has no safe setting: a 1.3x or 1.45x regression on the project's own tail agent is tightened in 10 of 10 seeds, and a 3x regression that starts at task 500 costs 17 points. The committed regressions were all 2 to 4x at tasks 3000 to 4500. | high | plausible (reviewer's runs; not independently rerun) | Recorded as an open limit; candidate for round 13. |
| T4 | Autocorrelated durations with identical marginals (AR(1), rho 0.9) move honest regressions from 1 to 2 of 10 tightened to 8 to 9 of 10; bursty 2.5x slow episodes 8% of the time take should-not tightenings from 30 to 95 of 160. The generator has no time structure. | high | plausible | Recorded as an open limit; candidate for round 13. |
| T5 | Ground truth: four should-tighten agents (leaks, bursts) cannot be touched by the deadline-only policy, so the hit rate is 5 of 9; the inflate attacker's own slowed tasks are counted as legitimate cost; the bridge already holds a survival extrapolation that could separate a natural tail from hangs and only wires it into the drift branch. | medium | confirmed by reading | Documented; the survival discriminator is a candidate change. |
| T6 | A slice is 22.6 hours of simulated time, so a diurnal cycle cancels inside it; with 2-hour slices and a ±30% cycle, honest regressions were tightened in every seed. | medium | plausible | Recorded. |
| T7 | Runaways hang from task 0 (breaker spent before a baseline exists); regressions start only after slice 12; pulses align to slice boundaries. | low | confirmed by reading | Recorded. |

## What survives

The reviewers were asked what would survive contact with real data. Their answer, which the author accepts: the store-enforced monotonicity and the audit chain; saved% as arithmetic but not as benefit; the cap bounding per-agent harm, at 2 to 10 points rather than near zero once deadlines are set with ordinary headroom; and the qualitative point that a bounded, signal-interpreting limiter protects honest agents where an unbounded one does not. What does not survive: "healthy agents are never tightened", "the drift hold protects regressions at 0.2 points", "the excess release is free", the ratchet-equals-capped-two-way equivalence as a general statement, every threshold as a default, and the claim that the one-way direction has a measured benefit on these traces.

## The standing of the thesis after this

Before the red team, the claim was: bounding automation and interpreting the signal are what protect honest agents; the one-way direction adds a narrow, formula-stated benefit against patient attackers; the human gate costs about one decision per twenty thousand tasks. After it: the first part stands with the headroom condition attached; the second part is withdrawn pending round 12, because the measured benefit came from a cold-start artefact and a baseline without hysteresis; the third part stands, with the 60 decisions on honest agents now broken out. The architecture's security invariant stands with two real fixes (S1, S2), and the default configuration was weaker than the documents said until S2.
